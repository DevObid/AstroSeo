## What Is ProfitMaps AI?

ProfitMaps AI is a local SEO and marketing platform designed around businesses that want to monitor and improve their visibility in local search.

The walkthrough starts inside a **Business Workspace**, where businesses can be managed from a central dashboard.

From there, the platform provides access to tools for:

* Local map scanning
* Keyword tracking
* Competitor analysis
* SEO action plans
* Content creation
* Google Business Profile content
* Website page planning
* Website creation
* Client reporting
* Recurring scans

The interesting part isn't necessarily any individual feature. It's the way the platform connects them together.

Instead of using one tool for ranking data, another for content, and another for client reports, ProfitMaps AI is attempting to put these activities into one workflow.

## ProfitMaps AI Walkthrough: How It Works

The video provides a good overview of the platform's workflow. Here's what happens.

### 1. Start With a Business Workspace

The first screen shows the ProfitMaps AI dashboard and its business workspace. A business can be selected and managed from the workspace.

The dashboard provides access to the different parts of the platform instead of requiring the user to jump between completely separate tools. This is particularly relevant for agencies or freelancers who may need to manage multiple local businesses.

The basic workflow starts with one question:

> **Where does this business appear in local search?**

That's where the map scan comes in.

### 2. Run a Local Map Scan

One of the main features demonstrated in the video is the **map scan**.

The user starts a new scan and enters a keyword that customers might use when searching for the business. Examples shown in the walkthrough include searches around local services such as:

* Plumber
* Plumbers
* Hair salon
* Hair stylist

The scan then creates a geographic grid around the business. Instead of showing one ranking number, the system checks multiple points across the surrounding area.

Local rankings can change depending on where the search is performed. A business might have strong visibility close to its location but weaker visibility farther away. A single ranking number can hide this — the map grid makes the difference much easier to see.

### 3. See Rankings Across the Local Area

The map interface is probably one of the most visually useful parts of the platform. The walkthrough shows a grid of ranking points displayed across the map.

Different ranking positions are represented visually, allowing the user to quickly identify areas where the business has stronger or weaker visibility.

This gives you a much more useful question than "What position does my business rank at?" You can instead ask: **"Where does my business rank, and how does that change across the local area?"**

For local SEO agencies, this can also make client reporting easier because the data is much more visual.

### 4. Compare the Business With Competitors

ProfitMaps AI doesn't stop at the client's own rankings. The walkthrough also demonstrates a **Competitor Overlay**.

The system identifies businesses appearing around the scanned area and allows the user to compare the local search landscape. This can reveal:

* Which businesses appear frequently
* Which competitors dominate certain areas
* Where the client's visibility is weaker
* Which locations may represent opportunities

Ranking data without competitive context can be difficult to interpret. Knowing that a business ranks #6 is one thing — knowing that several competitors consistently appear above it gives the ranking much more context.

### 5. Generate an SEO Action Plan

This is one of the more interesting parts of the walkthrough.

After collecting ranking information, ProfitMaps AI can generate an **Action Plan**. Instead of simply presenting numbers, the system turns the information into recommendations.

The walkthrough shows the platform analyzing the existing visibility of a business and suggesting potential next steps, which can involve areas such as:

* Expanding keyword coverage
* Improving local visibility
* Targeting additional service keywords
* Improving Google Business Profile activity
* Creating additional content
* Strengthening local relevance

This changes the workflow from "Here are your rankings" to "Here is what you could work on next" — an important distinction for anyone selling SEO as a service.

### 6. Create Local SEO Content

ProfitMaps AI also includes an AI-powered content workflow.

The walkthrough demonstrates the **Page Articles** section, where users can create content around a target keyword. The interface provides controls for things such as:

* Target keyword
* Additional keywords
* Minimum word count
* Maximum word count

The walkthrough then generates an article around a local service topic — for example, a service such as **Water Heater Repair in Ocoee, FL**. The generated content is structured around the selected topic and keywords.

For agencies, the potential advantage is speed. Instead of starting every local service article from an empty document, the platform can provide a first draft that can then be reviewed and edited.

### 7. Google Business Profile Content

The platform also includes a workflow for creating **Google Business Profile posts**. The walkthrough shows the user creating content for a business profile rather than simply focusing on website pages.

This is useful because local SEO isn't limited to website content. A complete local marketing strategy can involve several assets, including:

**Website + Google Business Profile + Local Content + Reviews + Local Visibility**

ProfitMaps AI attempts to bring some of these activities into the same workflow. One important distinction, however, is that the walkthrough demonstrates **content preparation**, not a claim that every generated post is automatically published to Google.

### 8. Build a Website Content Plan

Another section shown in the video is the **Site Plan**. This takes the SEO research and connects it to website pages.

Instead of simply generating random blog posts, the platform can organize pages around a business's services and target keywords, mapping:

**Service → Keyword → Website Page**

For a local business, that could mean having dedicated pages for different services rather than trying to rank one generic homepage for everything — particularly useful for businesses with several services.

### 9. Review the Website Before Launch

The walkthrough then moves into the website-building part of the platform. The interface allows the user to review business details and planned pages before moving forward.

The concept is essentially: **Research first → Build second**. Rather than creating a generic website and thinking about SEO afterward, the platform connects the website structure with the local SEO information gathered earlier.

### 10. Generated Website Example

The walkthrough also shows an example of a generated website, with elements such as:

* Hero section
* Services
* Business information
* Questions/FAQ content
* Calls to action
* Contact functionality

This demonstrates how ProfitMaps AI goes beyond being purely a rank tracker. However, the website-building functionality should be evaluated separately from the SEO features. A generated website can provide a starting point, but businesses should still review branding, content accuracy, business information, service descriptions, conversion flow, SEO metadata, and mobile experience before publishing.

### 11. Recurring SEO Scans

Another feature demonstrated toward the end of the walkthrough is **recurring scanning**. Instead of manually launching a new scan every time, users can configure recurring scans — the example shown uses a weekly schedule.

This is useful for ongoing SEO monitoring because local rankings can change over time. The workflow becomes:

**Initial Scan → SEO Work → Wait → Rescan → Compare**

That makes more sense for an ongoing SEO service than running one scan and never checking the business again.

## ProfitMaps AI Uses Credits

One thing that stood out in the walkthrough is the platform's use of **credits**. The interface shows credit requirements when running scans and configuring recurring scans.

This means credit consumption should be part of your evaluation. For example, an agency managing 5, 10, 25, or 50+ businesses will have very different scanning requirements. The number of keywords and scan frequency will also affect usage.

So before choosing a plan, it's worth calculating your expected monthly workload rather than looking only at the headline feature list.
